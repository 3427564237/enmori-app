# LI — original service recovery after the synthetic evaluation slot

28 October 2026, 16:00 SGT. Scope: restore the existing local
`enmori-group.service` under the single-use authorization in
[LI's decision](../../decisions/2026-10-28-li-ai-repair-and-azure-setup.md).
This is service readiness evidence, not AI semantic, cold-start, phone, cloud,
UAT or release acceptance.

## Diagnosis

- The original service had repeatedly exited with its generic startup error.
  Its catch did not identify the failing stage. In an independent read-only
  probe using the same Firebase composition, the Auth Admin verification-user
  read failed after approximately 44.9 seconds. No identity, token or provider
  error text was recorded. This probe isolates a failing dependency boundary;
  the generic service log does not independently prove that every prior exit
  failed at that exact point.
- The host's first two configured DNS resolvers timed out during individual
  three-second queries; the existing third and fourth IPv6 resolvers answered
  in approximately 2–3 ms. Normal host lookups took approximately eight
  seconds. After lookup, direct IPv4 and IPv6 HTTPS probes to the Auth endpoint
  both reached the server. The host resolver file is generated by
  NetworkManager; no resolver address or system-wide network setting was
  changed.
- With only `RES_OPTIONS=timeout:1 attempts:1` applied to a separate probe
  process, DNS lookup took approximately two seconds, the same Auth Admin
  read completed in 9.27 seconds, and its existing Firestore binding read
  completed approximately 2.22 seconds later. This demonstrates a useful
  mitigation under the observed conditions, not future DNS reliability.

## Authorized one-time lock recovery

After the service was stopped while still starting, its process lock remained.
Before recovery, the unit was inactive, the recorded PID was absent, ports
9381 and 9382 had no listeners, and the valid owner-only 0600 lock was a single
link from the same boot. The configured first-flow worker and push were both
absent. A read-only aggregate of `ai_operation_fences` returned zero documents
at that point; it is not proof about every historical or external operation.

The existing automatic guard rejects a same-boot stale lock. LI authorized this
specific incident recovery without changing that standing guard. The exact
original lock inode and contents were atomically moved with
`renameat2(RENAME_NOREPLACE)` to the owner-only evidence directory below; its
SHA-256 is `860a62fea68c8ccb2e658d00b151782e0acd4e3af7f73412f42d8155f82b121b`.
No AI operation fence was manually changed or removed. The normal startup may
perform its existing runtime-epoch and maintenance operations.
